# Test results, 22 November 2026

Two tests on the phone website: naming objects in real photos, and reading text
from pages. All scores are from the code as it was on this date.

## 1. Naming objects in real photos

**Photos:** 22 phone photos of everyday objects at home, one main object each
(`photos/`, labels in `labels.csv`). Two more were left out: raw chicken (not an
object ClearView knows) and one photo too blurry to label. `wallet-open.jpg` shows
ID and bank cards, so it is kept off GitHub (`.gitignore`); ask the author for it.

**How it was scored:** right if the app's main answer names the labelled object.
"Mouse" counts for "computer mouse", and "ceiling light" for "light bulb".

| Set-up | Right | Script |
|---|---|---|
| On-device detector only (no internet) | 4/22 (18%) | `evaluate.py --compare`, `results-combined.csv` |
| Full app, before the fixes below | 9/22 (41%) | `cloud_check.py` |
| Full app, after fix A | 14/22 (64%) | `cloud_check.py` |
| **Full app, after fixes A and B (live)** | **20/22 (91%)** | `cloud_check.py`, `results-cloud.csv` |

The two remaining misses are both pears, named "yellow fruit".

**Fixes made from this test:**
- **A. Send the whole photo when the detector's pick looks wrong.** If the chosen
  box covers less than 10% of the photo, or is a person, the cloud sees the whole
  photo instead of a crop. This fixed the open wallet (the detector had picked a
  keyboard corner), the laptop, the fan and one pair of glasses.
- **B. Take the right name from the cloud's description.** "This is a stainless
  steel refrigerator" had been shortened to "This". Also removed "a tea bottle" from
  the question sent to the cloud: the model had been repeating it ("a yellow tea
  bottle" for a pear).
- Furniture (desk, table, bed, sofa) is no longer chosen as the main object. This
  changed some answers ("desk" became "cup") but not the score.

**Limits, to state in the report:**
- The 10% limit in fix A was chosen by looking at these same 22 photos, so 91% is
  likely higher than the score on new photos. A fresh set of 30–50 photos is the fair test.
- With 22 photos, 20/22 has a 95% confidence interval of roughly 72–97%.
- The detector-only score (4/22) was measured with the desktop app's rule for
  choosing the main object. The phone's rule is slightly simpler (it never picks a
  person, and has no "held in a hand" rule), so its detector-only score may differ a little.
- Most misses came from the photos: close-ups that fill the frame, blur, dim light,
  busy patterned backgrounds, and unusual angles.

## 2. Reading text from pages

**Pages:** five test pages made for this test by `text-pages/make_test_pages.py`
(original text; correct text in `text-pages/truth.json`), plus two pages from
published books photographed by the author. The two book pages are kept off GitHub
(copyright); only their scores are recorded here.

**How it was scored:** share of characters read correctly. Each reading is compared
with the correct text by edit distance, counting letters, numbers and Chinese
characters only, not spaces or punctuation. Run in Chrome with the live site's text
reader (Tesseract.js 6).

### 2a. Evening out the lighting before reading

The app now evens out the page's brightness before reading (shadows and coloured
backgrounds become white). Traditional Chinese setting; all characters counted.

| Page | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Printed leaflet, English + 繁體 (`ocr-test-page.png`) | 97% | 98% |
| Same leaflet as a phone photo: tilted, shadow, blur (`ocr-phone-photo.jpg`) | 98% | 98% |
| English storybook page (`story-en.png`) | 100% | 100% |
| Chinese storybook page, 標楷體 (`story-zh.png`) | 93% | 91% |
| **Open picture book photo: fold shadow, text over the picture (`story-photo.jpg`)** | **63%** | **97%** |
| Eraser label / banknote (object photos) | nothing found | nothing found |

Labels on objects are not read by the text scanner. The object scanner reads them
instead (it read "Faber-Castell", "DUST-FREE" on the eraser).

### 2b. Choosing the kind of Chinese

Chinese characters only, after 2a. "Both" loads Traditional and Simplified at once.

| Page | Traditional | Simplified | Both |
|---|---|---|---|
| Published book page, Simplified Chinese (author's photo, 338 characters) | 76% | **99%** | 98% |
| Storybook page, Traditional 標楷體 (`story-zh.png`) | **91%** | 77% | 86% |
| Leaflet, Traditional 正黑體 (`ocr-test-page.png`) | **90%** | 71% | 81% |
| Open-book photo, Traditional (`story-photo.jpg`) | **95%** | 74% | **95%** |

Reading a page with the wrong kind of Chinese costs about 20 points, and loading
both loses 5–10 points on Traditional pages. So the scanner now has a language
choice (English / 繁體中文 / 简体中文, default 繁體中文).

The Traditional pages above have only 20–56 Chinese characters, so one mistake moves
their score by 2–5 points.

### 2c. A children's book with pinyin

A published children's picture book page (author's photo): Simplified Chinese, pinyin
above every line, text wrapped around pictures. 172 Chinese characters.

| Set-up | Chinese characters right |
|---|---|
| Traditional, before 2a | 10% |
| Traditional, after 2a (automatic page layout) | 0% (nothing read) |
| Traditional, "single block" layout | 35% |
| Simplified, "single block" layout | 59%, mixed with garbled pinyin lines |
| Cloud model (Moondream) | named it "a page of Chinese text", read almost none |

Pages with pinyin or 注音 above the characters are a known limit. Tesseract can't
separate the small pronunciation lines from the text. A Chinese-specific reader such
as PaddleOCR would be needed to fix this.

## 3. Supermarket test (added 24–25 November)

**Photos:** 32 phone photos taken in a Carrefour supermarket in Taiwan
(`photos-store/`, labels in `labels-store.csv`). These were new photos that had not
been used for any of the fixes above. 27 were scored. Five were left out: three
carbon heaters, a light-bulb box and a night light. For the hot dog bun, "bread" or
"hot dog" both count as right.

**How it was scored:** the same way as test 1 (`cloud_check.py`), with one addition
made during this test: "milk" counts for "milk carton".

| Code version | Supermarket (27) | Home (22, test 1) | File |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Before any fix (held-out test)** | **7/27 (26%)** | 20/22 | `results-store.csv` |
| After fix C (5060aeb8) | 15/27 (56%) | 20/22 | `results-store-after.csv`, `results-cloud-after.csv` |
| After fix D (befd014c) | 17/27 (63%) | 20/22 | `results-store-after2.csv`, `results-cloud-after2.csv` |

**Fixes made from this test:**
- **C. Name what is inside the packaging.** The cloud's description named the
  product, but the app had taken the container's name ("plastic bag", "package",
  "box", "stack"). The app now uses the product's name: "a plastic bag of tomatoes"
  gives "tomato". Plurals are matched ("toothbrushes"), and "chips" was added so
  "potato chips" isn't named "potato".
- **D. Don't take a colour or a hand as the object.** "Orange", "peach", "lemon" and
  "milk" name the object only when they come last ("Koko milk chocolate" is not
  milk, and "bright orange object" is not an orange). "A hand holding X" is named by X.

**Limits, to state in the report:**
- **26% is the fair score on new photos.** Fixes C and D were made while looking at
  these same photos, so 56% and 63% show the fixes worked here, not how the app
  does on new photos. The home score stayed at 20/22, so the fixes broke nothing there.
- With 27 photos, 7/27 has a 95% confidence interval of roughly 13–45%.
- The vision model can't recognise some items: Asian pears ("round, yellowish
  fruit"), carrots in plastic ("candy"), bread loaves, a cereal box named after its
  brand ("Koko milk chocolate"), and Darlie toothpaste ("breath strips").
- Two answers are reasonable but don't match the label, so they score as wrong:
  "chips" (labelled "snack bag") and "AnSung noodle soup" (labelled "noodles").
- After fix D, two wrong names became vague ones: one pear photo had been
  called "bread" and one carrot "orange". A vague but true answer is safer for a
  reader than a wrong name, but it doesn't raise the score.
